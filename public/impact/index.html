How impact is scored

Each exhibit is scored 0 to 10 on five dimensions, using only facts from its named public source. The overall impact is the plain average of the five, to one decimal place. The home page draws it as five dots, one dot for every 2 points.

Scores sit on the levels below. An odd score means the facts fall between two levels.

The score measures how far an outage reached, not who was at fault.

Reach

Who was directly affected

ScoreMeaning
0No outside users
2Inside one organization
4One product's users in one region
6One product's users worldwide
8Several major services, worldwide
10Across industries, at the level of society

Duration

Time until the main service was back

ScoreMeaning
0Under 5 minutes
25 to 30 minutes
430 minutes to 2 hours
62 to 8 hours
88 to 48 hours
10Over 48 hours, or never restored

Loss

What was lost for good

ScoreMeaning
0Nothing
2A small cost that can be recovered
4Lost revenue stated in the source, no data lost
6Some data lost for good, or a stated loss of $10 million or more
8A lot of data lost for good, or a stated loss of $100 million or more
10The whole asset lost: vehicle destroyed, mission failed

Recovery

How hard it was to recover

ScoreMeaning
0Recovered on its own
2One-click rollback or config revert
4Remote manual intervention
6Several teams, systems rebuilt
8On-site access, or fixing machines one by one
10Cannot be fixed, only replaced

Cascade

How far it spread beyond the system that failed

ScoreMeaning
0No spillover
2Only the owner's own dependent systems
4A few customers or partners
6Many downstream sites of one provider
8Large parts of the internet, or several industries
10Flights, hospitals or emergency lines halted at scale
